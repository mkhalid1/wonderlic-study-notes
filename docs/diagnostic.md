# 10-minute diagnostic

Twelve original items, two of each question type, to show you where to start. Set a timer for **3 minutes** for all 12. That is close to the real test's pace of about 14 seconds a question. Answer everything without a calculator, then use the remaining time to check your answers and read the table at the bottom.

These items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from the Wonderlic or any publisher's materials.

If you already know the question types, skip this and take the [free full-length Wonderlic practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/wonderlic?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=wonderlic) instead. A full 50-question run gives a better picture of pacing.

## Part A: Math and word problems

**D1.** A recipe for 4 people uses 300 g of flour. How much flour is needed for 6 people?

A. 350 g &nbsp; B. 400 g &nbsp; C. 450 g &nbsp; D. 500 g &nbsp; E. 600 g

**D2.** A car uses 8 litres of fuel per 100 km. How many litres does it use for 250 km?

A. 20 &nbsp; B. 22 &nbsp; C. 24 &nbsp; D. 25 &nbsp; E. 32

## Part B: Number and letter series

What comes next?

**D3.** 1, 3, 9, 27, ?

A. 36 &nbsp; B. 54 &nbsp; C. 81 &nbsp; D. 108

**D4.** B, D, G, K, ?

A. O &nbsp; B. P &nbsp; C. Q &nbsp; D. R &nbsp; E. S

## Part C: Vocabulary and word meaning

**D5.** VIVID and DULL. Do these words have:

A. similar meanings &nbsp; B. opposite meanings &nbsp; C. neither similar nor opposite meanings

**D6.** "She seldom misses a deadline." Which statement means the same?

A. She often misses deadlines. &nbsp; B. She never misses deadlines. &nbsp; C. She rarely misses deadlines. &nbsp; D. She sets her own deadlines.

## Part D: Analogies

**D7.** BIRD is to NEST as BEE is to:

A. honey &nbsp; B. hive &nbsp; C. flower &nbsp; D. wing &nbsp; E. queen

**D8.** WATER is to THIRST as FOOD is to:

A. hunger &nbsp; B. kitchen &nbsp; C. meal &nbsp; D. taste &nbsp; E. plate

## Part E: Logic puzzles

Assume the first two statements are true. Is the third statement **A. true**, **B. false** or **C. not certain**?

**D9.** Some clerks are part-time. Jo is a clerk. Jo is part-time.

**D10.** Ann is older than Bea. Bea is older than Cy. Cy is older than Ann.

## Part F: Spatial reasoning

**D11.** A square sheet of paper is folded in half once. Two holes are punched through both layers. When it is unfolded, how many holes are there?

A. 2 &nbsp; B. 3 &nbsp; C. 4 &nbsp; D. 6 &nbsp; E. 8

**D12.** A cube is painted on all six faces and cut into 8 equal small cubes (2 × 2 × 2). How many small cubes have paint on exactly three faces?

A. 0 &nbsp; B. 2 &nbsp; C. 4 &nbsp; D. 6 &nbsp; E. 8

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, D1 to D12</summary>

**D1: C (450 g).** 6 people is one and a half times 4 people, and 300 × 1.5 = 450.

**D2: A (20).** 250 km is two and a half lots of 100 km. 8 × 2.5 = 20.

**D3: C (81).** Each term is three times the one before. 27 × 3 = 81. *Why not B:* 54 doubles 27.

**D4: B (P).** As numbers: B = 2, D = 4, G = 7, K = 11. The gaps are 2, 3, 4, so the next gap is 5, and 11 + 5 = 16, which is P.

**D5: B (opposite).** Vivid means bright and striking. Dull means lacking brightness or interest.

**D6: C.** "Seldom" means rarely. *Why not B:* "never" overstates it.

**D7: B (hive).** A bird lives in a nest, and a bee lives in a hive. *Why not A:* bees make honey, but that is a different relationship.

**D8: A (hunger).** Water relieves thirst, and food relieves hunger.

**D9: C (not certain).** Only some clerks are part-time. Jo may or may not be one of them.

**D10: B (false).** Ann is older than Bea, who is older than Cy, so Ann is older than Cy. Cy cannot be older than Ann.

**D11: C (4).** One fold makes 2 layers. Each of the 2 punches goes through both layers, so the opened sheet has 2 × 2 = 4 holes.

**D12: E (8).** In a 2 × 2 × 2 cube, every small cube sits at a corner of the large cube, so every one shows three painted faces.

</details>

## What your result means

Count your correct answers in each part.

| Part | Your score | If you scored 0 | If you scored 1 | If you scored 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Math | /2 | Start with the [math guide](sections/math-word-problems.md) | Do its items, focus on the traps list | Light review only |
| B. Series | /2 | Start with the [series guide](sections/number-series.md) | Practise writing the gaps every time | Light review only |
| C. Vocabulary | /2 | Start with the [vocabulary guide](sections/vocabulary.md) | Practise reading the instruction word first | Light review only |
| D. Analogies | /2 | Start with the [analogies guide](sections/analogies.md) | Practise the linking sentence | Light review only |
| E. Logic | /2 | Start with the [logic guide](sections/logic.md) | Learn the patterns table | Light review only |
| F. Spatial | /2 | Start with the [spatial guide](sections/spatial-reasoning.md) | Sketch every item | Light review only |

Also look at the clock. If you finished all 12 well inside 3 minutes, your accuracy matters more than your speed. If you ran out of time, read the [timing guide](timing.md) early in your plan.

Then pick a plan: the [7-day plan](study-plans.md#7-day-plan) if you have a week, or the [2-day plan](study-plans.md#2-day-plan) if the test is close.
