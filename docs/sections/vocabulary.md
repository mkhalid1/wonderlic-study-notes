# Vocabulary and word meaning

**In one line:** you pick the word closest in meaning or opposite in meaning to a given word, decide whether two words are similar, opposite or neither, or choose what a short sentence really says.

Wonderlic lists "vocabulary" and "language comprehension" among the topics on its cognitive test, and its candidate page uses a "most nearly means" question as a sample. These items are short, so they are some of the quickest marks on the paper if your vocabulary is solid, and some of the quickest losses if you read too fast.

## What it measures

Two things, closely linked:

- **Word knowledge.** Do you know what common and moderately formal words mean, such as "prudent", "candid" or "mitigate"?
- **Careful reading.** Do you notice whether the question asks for the same meaning or the opposite, and do you read a sentence for what it says rather than what you expect?

Neither needs specialist knowledge. These are words you meet in a newspaper, a contract or a work email.

## How the questions look

| Format | What you do |
|---|---|
| Closest meaning | "Which word is closest in meaning to CANDID?" Pick one of five. |
| Opposite meaning | "Which word is most nearly opposite to FRUGAL?" |
| Similar, opposite or neither | "ADAPT and ADOPT. Do these words have similar meanings, opposite meanings, or neither?" |
| Odd one out | Four words share a meaning and one does not. |
| Sentence completion | Pick the word that best fills a gap in a sentence. |
| Sentence meaning | Pick the option that says the same thing as a short sentence. |

## A method that works

1. **Read the instruction word first.** Circle it mentally: closest, opposite, similar, neither. Most wrong answers in this section are right answers to a different question.
2. **Define the word in your own words before you look at the options.** "Prudent: careful, sensible with risk." Then find the option that matches your definition.
3. **If you do not know the word, use its parts or a familiar phrase.** "Mitigating circumstances" tells you "mitigate" means to make less severe.
4. **Eliminate the near misses.** Options are often related to the word without meaning it. "Hidden" is related to "candid" in topic, but means close to the opposite.
5. **For sentence meaning, restate it plainly.** "Hardly anyone came" becomes "very few came". Then pick the option that says exactly that, no stronger and no weaker.

## Traps that cost points

- **Answering "closest" when the item says "opposite".** The test usually includes the synonym among the options of an opposite question, and the antonym among the options of a synonym question.
- **Lookalike words.** Adapt and adopt, affect and effect, eminent and imminent. Read every letter.
- **Choosing a related word instead of an equivalent one.** "Thrifty" is related to "frugal" in topic, which makes it a trap on an opposite question: it means nearly the same.
- **Overstating a sentence.** "Hardly anyone" is not "nobody". "Seldom" is not "never". Pick the option with the same strength.
- **Missing a negative.** "Anything but brief" means long. "Not uncommon" means fairly common.

## Timing

As guidance: aim for 5 to 10 seconds on a vocabulary item. You either know the word or you can narrow it to two options quickly. If you are still stuck between two after 15 seconds, pick the one that fits your own definition best and move on. Thinking longer rarely helps on word knowledge.

## Practice items

These 5 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from the Wonderlic or any publisher's materials. A fair target is 5 items in 50 seconds.

**Item 1.** Which word is most nearly opposite in meaning to FRUGAL?

A. thrifty &nbsp; B. poor &nbsp; C. wasteful &nbsp; D. cautious &nbsp; E. simple

**Item 2.** Choose the word that best completes the sentence: "The new manager was so ___ that she remembered every employee's name after a single meeting."

A. careless &nbsp; B. timid &nbsp; C. reluctant &nbsp; D. observant &nbsp; E. vague

**Item 3.** Which word does not belong with the others?

A. rapid &nbsp; B. sluggish &nbsp; C. swift &nbsp; D. brisk &nbsp; E. quick

**Item 4.** ADAPT and ADOPT. Do these words have:

A. similar meanings &nbsp; B. opposite meanings &nbsp; C. neither similar nor opposite meanings

**Item 5.** "Hardly anyone came to the meeting." Which statement means the same?

A. Many people came to the meeting. &nbsp; B. The meeting was difficult. &nbsp; C. Very few people came to the meeting. &nbsp; D. Nobody came to the meeting. &nbsp; E. The meeting was cancelled.

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 5</summary>

**Item 1: C (wasteful).** Frugal means careful with money and resources. The opposite is wasteful. *Why not A:* "thrifty" means nearly the same as frugal, which makes it the classic trap on an opposite question.

**Item 2: D (observant).** Remembering every name after one meeting shows that she notices and takes in details. *Why not E:* "vague" would mean the opposite. The others do not fit the praise in the sentence.

**Item 3: B (sluggish).** Rapid, swift, brisk and quick all mean fast. Sluggish means slow.

**Item 4: C (neither).** To adapt is to change to suit new conditions. To adopt is to take something on as your own, such as a plan or a child. The words look alike but their meanings are unrelated, not opposite. *Why not A:* the shared letters make them look similar, which is the trap.

**Item 5: C.** "Hardly anyone" means very few people, not none. *Why not D:* "nobody" overstates it. A few people did come.

</details>

## Score yourself

| Item | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Answer | C | D | B | C | C |

- **5:** keep it fast. These are your cheapest marks.
- **4:** check which kind of miss it was. If you answered the wrong instruction (same versus opposite), slow down on the first word of every item. If you did not know the word, the fix is reading, below.
- **3 or fewer:** a vocabulary gap takes longer to close than a method gap. Read a quality newspaper or long-form articles for 20 minutes a day and note any word you could not define. Meanwhile, the instruction-word habit in step 1 still saves marks immediately.
