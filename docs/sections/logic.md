# Logic puzzles

**In one line:** you take a few short statements as true and decide what must follow: whether a conclusion is true, false or not certain, who finishes in which place, or who sits where.

Wonderlic lists "basic logic" and "logic puzzles" among its test topics. Its candidate FAQ describes the timed questions as measuring "your ability to apply logic and reason to select an answer", not knowledge. Logic items are where that is most literal.

## What it measures

Whether you can reason strictly from what you are told. Three habits matter:

- treating the statements as true, even if they sound odd
- not adding facts the statements do not give you
- telling "must be true" apart from "could be true"

## How the questions look

The most common shape gives two statements and a third to judge:

> The first two statements are true. Is the third statement true, false, or not certain?

Others are short puzzles: a race order, a row of seats, people who like tea or coffee. They have one right answer that you can prove from the facts.

The three labels in the true, false, not certain format:

| Label | Choose it when |
|---|---|
| **True** | The third statement must be true whenever the first two are. |
| **False** | The third statement cannot be true if the first two are. |
| **Not certain** | The third statement could be true or false. The first two do not settle it. |

## A method that works

1. **Translate each statement into a picture or a short note.** "All managers drive" becomes "managers inside drivers". "Tom is taller than Sam" becomes "T > S".
2. **Ask "must it?" not "could it?".** For a conclusion to be true, there must be no possible situation where the statements hold and the conclusion fails.
3. **Try to break the conclusion.** Invent a case that fits the statements but makes the conclusion false. If you can, the answer is not certain (or false, if every case breaks it).
4. **For ordering and seating puzzles, place the fixed facts first.** "Dee was first" and "Eve was last" go down before any "before" or "after" clues.
5. **Check the answer against every clue** before you click. One overlooked clue is the usual cause of a wrong seating answer.

Some patterns worth knowing by heart:

| Statements | Conclusion | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| All A are B. X is an A. | X is a B. | True |
| All A are B. X is not a B. | X is an A. | False |
| All A are B. X is a B. | X is an A. | Not certain |
| If P then Q. Not Q. | Not P. | True |
| If P then Q. Q. | P. | Not certain |
| Some A are B. All B are C. | Some A are C. | True |
| All A are B. Some B are C. | Some A are C. | Not certain |

## Traps that cost points

- **Reversing "all".** "All roses are flowers" does not mean all flowers are roses. "All A are B, X is a B" never proves X is an A.
- **Affirming the result.** "If it rains, the match is cancelled. The match was cancelled." It may have been cancelled for another reason, so you cannot conclude it rained.
- **Reading "some" as "only some".** In logic, "some" means at least one, and it could be all.
- **Bringing in real-world knowledge.** If the statements say "all fish can fly", work with that.
- **Choosing "false" when you mean "not proved".** False needs the conclusion to clash with the statements. If it simply does not follow, the answer is not certain.

## Timing

As guidance: a two-statement item should take 10 to 15 seconds once you know the patterns table. Ordering and seating puzzles take longer, often 25 to 40 seconds. If a puzzle has more than four people or more than four clues, consider it a candidate for a quick best guess, since the time could buy two or three easier marks further on.

## Practice items

These 5 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from the Wonderlic or any publisher's materials. For items with A, B and C only, choose A for true, B for false and C for not certain. A fair target is 5 items in 1 minute 40 seconds.

For items 1, 2 and 4, assume the first two statements are true. Is the third statement **A. true**, **B. false** or **C. not certain**?

**Item 1.** All roses are flowers. Some flowers fade quickly. Some roses fade quickly.

**Item 2.** Tom is taller than Sam. Sam is taller than Lee. Lee is taller than Tom.

**Item 3.** Five runners finished a race. Dee finished first and Eve finished last. Ana finished ahead of Ben, and Cal finished behind Ben. Who finished third?

A. Ana &nbsp; B. Ben &nbsp; C. Cal &nbsp; D. Dee &nbsp; E. Eve

**Item 4.** If it rains, the match is cancelled. The match was cancelled. It rained.

**Item 5.** In a group of 30 people, 18 like tea, 15 like coffee and 6 like both. How many like neither?

A. 0 &nbsp; B. 3 &nbsp; C. 6 &nbsp; D. 9 &nbsp; E. 12

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 5</summary>

**Item 1: C (not certain).** The flowers that fade quickly might all be tulips. Nothing links them to roses. *Why not A:* "some flowers" may or may not include any roses.

**Item 2: B (false).** Tom is taller than Sam, who is taller than Lee, so Tom is taller than Lee. Lee cannot also be taller than Tom.

**Item 3: B (Ben).** Dee takes 1st and Eve takes 5th, which leaves places 2, 3 and 4 for Ana, Ben and Cal. Ana is ahead of Ben and Cal is behind Ben, so the order is Ana 2nd, Ben 3rd, Cal 4th.

**Item 4: C (not certain).** Rain is one thing that cancels the match, but the statements do not say it is the only one. The match could have been cancelled because of a strike or a waterlogged pitch. *Why not A:* this is the "affirming the result" trap in the patterns table.

**Item 5: B (3).** People who like at least one drink: 18 + 15 = 33, but the 6 who like both have been counted twice, so the real number is 33 − 6 = 27. That leaves 30 − 27 = 3 who like neither. *Why not A:* 0 comes from forgetting the double count, which seems to give 33 people in a group of 30.

</details>

## Score yourself

| Item | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Answer | C | B | B | C | B |

- **5:** your logic is sound. Keep the puzzles quick by always placing fixed facts first.
- **4:** check whether your misses were "not certain" items you marked true. That means you judged what could be true instead of what must be.
- **3 or fewer:** learn the patterns table until you can recite it, then redo items 1, 2 and 4, which all follow one of its rows.
