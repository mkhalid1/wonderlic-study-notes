# Number and letter series

**In one line:** you see a short sequence of numbers or letters and pick the one that comes next, or the one that breaks the pattern.

Series questions are some of the fastest marks on the Wonderlic once you know the handful of patterns they use. Wonderlic names "number series" and "pattern identification" among its topics, and its own candidate page uses a number series as a sample question.

## What it measures

A series question tests whether you can find a rule from a few examples and apply it one step further. That is close to the core of what Wonderlic says its cognitive test measures: how you learn and solve problems. There is no knowledge involved beyond counting and the alphabet.

## How the questions look

A row of four to seven terms, then a blank. Sometimes the question asks which term does not fit instead. Terms can be:

- whole numbers: 4, 9, 19, 39, ?
- letters: A, C, F, J, ?
- letter and number pairs: AB2, CD4, EF6, ?

Most series on tests like this use one of a small set of rules:

| Rule | What it looks like | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Add or subtract the same amount | Equal gaps | 7, 12, 17, 22 (add 5) |
| Multiply or divide by the same amount | Gaps grow fast | 3, 6, 12, 24 (double) |
| Gaps that change by a pattern | Gaps of 1, 2, 3, 4 or 2, 4, 8, 16 | 1, 2, 4, 7, 11 |
| Two series woven together | Up, down, up, down | 2, 10, 4, 11, 6, 12 |
| Multiply then add | Gaps double plus a bit | 1, 3, 7, 15 (double, add 1) |
| Squares and cubes | 1, 4, 9, 16 or 1, 8, 27 | |
| Each term from the two before | Fibonacci style | 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8 |

For letters, convert to numbers: A = 1, B = 2 and so on to Z = 26. It is worth knowing a few anchors by heart: E = 5, J = 10, O = 15, T = 20.

## A method that works

1. **Write the gaps.** Under the series, note the difference between each pair of terms. This one habit solves most items.
2. **Look at the gaps, not the terms.** Are they equal? Growing by the same amount? Doubling? If the gaps have their own pattern, extend it.
3. **If the gaps make no sense, check for two series.** Look at every other term: 1st, 3rd, 5th, and then 2nd, 4th, 6th.
4. **If the terms grow very fast, try multiplying.** Divide each term by the one before. A steady ratio means a multiply rule.
5. **Test your rule on every term,** not just the last two. A rule that fits only the end is a guess.
6. **Check the answer against the options.** If your number is not there, your rule is close but wrong. Recheck the first gap.

## Traps that cost points

- **Fitting only the last two terms.** In 2, 3, 5, 9, 17, the last gap is 8, but the rule is "gaps double": 1, 2, 4, 8, then 16.
- **Miscounting letters.** Counting on your fingers from A wastes seconds and invites errors. Use the anchors E, J, O, T and count from the nearest one.
- **Forgetting that a series can go down.** Z, X, V, T is a perfectly good series: subtract 2 each time.
- **Missing a woven series.** If the numbers jump up and down, it is usually two simple series taking turns, not one complicated rule.
- **"Which does not fit" items.** Find the rule using the terms that agree, then the odd one out is the term that breaks it. Do not look for the strangest-looking number.

## Timing

As guidance: a series item should take 10 to 15 seconds. Writing the gaps takes 3 or 4 of those, and it is time well spent. If the gaps, the alternating check and the ratio check all fail within about 20 seconds, guess and move on. Series items that resist all three checks are rare, and they are usually worth less than the easy items further on.

## Practice items

These 5 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from the Wonderlic or any publisher's materials. Choose the term that comes next unless the item says otherwise. A fair target is 5 items in 1 minute 15 seconds.

**Item 1.** 5, 8, 6, 9, 7, ?

A. 5 &nbsp; B. 8 &nbsp; C. 9 &nbsp; D. 10 &nbsp; E. 11

**Item 2.** 2, 3, 5, 9, 17, ?

A. 33 &nbsp; B. 34 &nbsp; C. 35 &nbsp; D. 36 &nbsp; E. 49

**Item 3.** A, C, F, J, ?

A. M &nbsp; B. N &nbsp; C. O &nbsp; D. P &nbsp; E. Q

**Item 4.** 4, 9, 19, 39, ?

A. 59 &nbsp; B. 69 &nbsp; C. 78 &nbsp; D. 79 &nbsp; E. 81

**Item 5.** In the series 3, 6, 12, 20, 48, 96, one term breaks the pattern. Which one?

A. 6 &nbsp; B. 12 &nbsp; C. 20 &nbsp; D. 48 &nbsp; E. 96

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 5</summary>

**Item 1: D (10).** The gaps go +3, −2, +3, −2, so the next is +3: 7 + 3 = 10. *Another way to see it:* two series take turns. The odd positions read 5, 6, 7 and the even positions read 8, 9, and the missing term is the next even one, 10.

**Item 2: A (33).** The gaps are 1, 2, 4, 8, doubling each time. The next gap is 16, and 17 + 16 = 33. The same series can be read as "double and subtract 1": 17 × 2 − 1 = 33. *Why not E:* 49 is not produced by any rule that fits every term.

**Item 3: C (O).** As numbers: A = 1, C = 3, F = 6, J = 10. The gaps are 2, 3, 4, so the next is 5. 10 + 5 = 15, which is O.

**Item 4: D (79).** Each term is double the one before plus 1: 4 × 2 + 1 = 9, 9 × 2 + 1 = 19, 19 × 2 + 1 = 39, and 39 × 2 + 1 = 79. *Check with gaps:* 5, 10, 20, then 40, and 39 + 40 = 79. *Why not C:* 78 doubles 39 but forgets the "+1".

**Item 5: C (20).** Five of the six terms fit "double each time": 3, 6, 12, 24, 48, 96. The term in the fourth place should be 24, not 20. *Why not D:* 48 looks like the odd jump because 20 to 48 is a big step, but 48 is exactly double the correct 24 and half of 96.

</details>
