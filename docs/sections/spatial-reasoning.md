# Spatial reasoning

**In one line:** you picture shapes, directions and objects in your head: turning them, folding them, counting their parts or following a route.

Wonderlic lists "spatial reasoning" and "pattern identification" among the topics on its cognitive test. On screen these items may come with a diagram. In these notes every item is described in words, so you can practise the same mental moves without pictures.

## What it measures

Whether you can hold a picture in your head and change it accurately: rotate it, flip it, fold and unfold it, or move through it. This matters in roles that read plans or layouts, and it is a general sign of how well you manage several pieces of information at once.

## How the questions look

| Kind | What you do |
|---|---|
| Directions | Follow a set of moves or turns and say where you end up, or which way you face |
| Rotation | Say what a shape, letter or clock hand looks like after a turn |
| Mirror images | Say what a word or shape looks like in a mirror |
| Folding and punching | Count the holes after a folded sheet is punched and opened |
| Counting | Count squares, triangles or faces in a figure |
| Cubes and dice | Work out painted faces or hidden faces |

## A method that works

1. **Draw it, if you can.** Wonderlic's pages do not say whether scratch paper is allowed, so check your invitation or ask the employer. Where it is, a 5-second sketch beats 20 seconds of picturing. Where it is not, trace directions with a finger on the desk.
2. **Fix one reference point.** For directions, keep north at the top of your sketch. For rotations, track one corner or one end of the shape.
3. **Break turns into quarter turns.** A 270° turn is three 90° turns. A half-turn is two. Turning right three times is the same as turning left once.
4. **For folding, count the layers.** Each fold in half doubles the layers. One punch through every layer makes as many holes as there are layers.
5. **For counting figures, count by size.** Count the smallest shapes first, then shapes made of two pieces, then larger ones. Write each count down.
6. **For cubes, think in positions.** On a painted cube cut into smaller cubes, corners have three painted faces, edges have two, face centres have one, and the inside has none.

## Traps that cost points

- **Mixing up your right and the page's right.** When you turn, "right" turns with you. Draw an arrow for the way you face and turn the arrow, not the page.
- **Forgetting that a mirror reverses the order of letters.** A word looks the same in a side mirror only if every letter is symmetric and the word reads the same backwards.
- **Undercounting figures.** The big shape that contains the small ones counts too.
- **Counting cube corners as edges.** A 3 × 3 × 3 cube has 12 edges with one middle cube each, plus 8 corner cubes that belong to no single edge.
- **Losing track of a long route.** Write each move as you read it: N3, E2, S3.

## Timing

As guidance: direction and rotation items take 10 to 15 seconds with a quick sketch. Counting items can take 20 or more. If a figure has too many lines to count in about 20 seconds, estimate, choose the nearest option and move on.

## Practice items

These 5 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from the Wonderlic or any publisher's materials. Use scratch paper while you practise. A fair target is 5 items in 1 minute 30 seconds.

**Item 1.** Which word looks exactly the same when you hold it up to a mirror placed beside it?

A. BOB &nbsp; B. DAD &nbsp; C. WOW &nbsp; D. NOON &nbsp; E. KAYAK

**Item 2.** You walk 3 blocks north, turn right and walk 2 blocks, then turn right again and walk 3 blocks. Where are you compared with where you started?

A. 2 blocks north &nbsp; B. 2 blocks east &nbsp; C. 2 blocks west &nbsp; D. 3 blocks south &nbsp; E. back at the start

**Item 3.** A large cube is painted on all six faces, then cut into 27 equal small cubes (3 × 3 × 3). How many small cubes have paint on exactly two faces?

A. 4 &nbsp; B. 6 &nbsp; C. 8 &nbsp; D. 12 &nbsp; E. 24

**Item 4.** A square sheet of paper is folded in half, then folded in half again. One hole is punched through all the layers. When the paper is unfolded, how many holes does it have?

A. 1 &nbsp; B. 2 &nbsp; C. 4 &nbsp; D. 6 &nbsp; E. 8

**Item 5.** A square has both of its diagonals drawn in. How many triangles of any size are there in the figure?

A. 4 &nbsp; B. 6 &nbsp; C. 8 &nbsp; D. 10 &nbsp; E. 12

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 5</summary>

**Item 1: C (WOW).** In a side mirror, each letter is flipped left to right and the order of the letters is reversed. W and O look the same when flipped, and WOW reads the same backwards, so the reflection matches. *Why not D:* NOON reads the same backwards, but a flipped N looks like a backwards N. *Why not A and B:* B and D are not symmetric left to right.

**Item 2: B (2 blocks east).** North 3, then a right turn faces you east for 2 blocks, then another right turn faces you south for 3 blocks. The north and south moves cancel, leaving you 2 blocks east of the start.

**Item 3: D (12).** Small cubes with exactly two painted faces sit in the middle of each edge of the large cube, one per edge. A cube has 12 edges. *Why not C:* the 8 corner cubes have three painted faces, not two.

**Item 4: C (4).** The first fold makes 2 layers and the second makes 4. A single punch goes through all 4, so the opened sheet has 4 holes.

**Item 5: C (8).** The two diagonals cut the square into 4 small triangles. Each diagonal on its own also splits the square into 2 large triangles, and there are 2 diagonals, which gives 4 large triangles. 4 + 4 = 8. *Why not A:* it counts only the smallest triangles.

## Score yourself

| Item | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Answer | C | B | D | C | C |

- **5:** spatial items are a strength. Your sketches are doing their job.
- **4:** look at which kind you missed. Counting misses usually mean the larger shapes were skipped. Direction misses usually mean "right" was taken from the page, not from the way you faced.
- **3 or fewer:** sketch every item for a week, even the easy ones, until fixing a reference point is automatic. Then redo this set.
