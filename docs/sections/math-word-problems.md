# Math and word problems

**In one line:** short arithmetic questions, often wrapped in a sentence about prices, speeds, wages or quantities, solved in your head without a calculator.

Math is the question type people fear most on the Wonderlic, mostly because of the no-calculator rule. The arithmetic itself is rarely harder than a few steps of multiplication or a percentage. What makes it hard is doing it in about 14 seconds while the clock runs.

## What it measures

Wonderlic lists "simple math" and "word problems" among the topics in its cognitive test. Its candidate FAQ is clear about what the math is for: the test "is not designed to test math skills, but rather to evaluate your reasoning abilities in arriving at answers," and only a small number of questions need real calculation.

So the skill being tested is not long division. It is:

- turning a sentence into the right operation (is this a rate, a ratio or a percentage?)
- spotting the shortcut that makes the numbers easy
- checking that the answer makes sense before you click

## How the questions look

A one- or two-sentence problem, then four or five answer choices. Typical shapes:

| Shape | Example in our own words |
|---|---|
| Percentages | A coat is marked down 25%. What does it cost now? |
| Rates | A van covers 120 miles in 2 hours. How far in 5 hours? |
| Ratios | Red to blue marbles is 2 to 3. There are 30 marbles. How many are blue? |
| Averages | Four test scores average 80. What score on a fifth test makes the average 82? |
| Work and time | One person does a job in 4 hours, another in 6. How long together? |
| Money and wages | Overtime, discounts, change from a purchase |

Prices on the test may come with a currency sign. In these notes we write them as plain numbers.

## A method that works

1. **Read the last line first.** Know what you are solving for (the original price, the number of girls, the total) before you read the numbers.
2. **Name the operation.** Say to yourself "this is a percent decrease" or "this is a rate." Most mistakes happen when you multiply where you should divide.
3. **Make the numbers friendly.** 15% is 10% plus half of 10%. Dividing by 0.8 is the same as multiplying by 1.25. 25% is a quarter.
4. **Estimate, then look at the options.** Often only one option is in the right range, and you are done without exact arithmetic.
5. **Sanity-check the direction.** An original price must be higher than the sale price. A combined work time must be shorter than either person's time alone.
6. **If it needs more than three steps, guess and move on.** Wonderlic's own tip is "make your best guess and move on" when a question takes too long.

## Traps that cost points

- **Taking a percentage of the wrong number.** If a price falls 20% to 48, the 20% was of the original, not of 48. So the original is 48 ÷ 0.8 = 60, not 48 + 9.6.
- **Assuming an increase and a decrease cancel.** Up 10% then down 10% leaves you 1% lower, because the second 10% is taken from a bigger number.
- **Rounding the wrong way.** If 250 items go into boxes of 24, you need 11 boxes, not 10. Real-world "how many do you need" questions round up.
- **Adding times in work problems.** Two people working together finish faster than either alone. If your answer is longer than the faster person's time, it is wrong.
- **Missing a unit change.** Minutes and hours, grams and kilograms, dozens and units. Convert before you calculate.
- **Doing the whole sum when the options give it away.** If the options are 12, 120, 1,200 and 12,000, you only need the size of the answer.

## Timing

Math questions vary more in length than any other type. A plain percentage can take 5 seconds, a two-equation word problem can take 40. As guidance: give a math item up to about 20 seconds. If you have not found a route to the answer by then, eliminate any option that is clearly wrong, pick from what is left and move on. Coming back later is not a safe plan, since Wonderlic's FAQ advises against skipping around.

## Practice items

These 5 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from the Wonderlic or any publisher's materials. Work them without a calculator. A fair target is 5 items in 1 minute 40 seconds.

**Item 1.** A jacket is marked down 20% and now costs 48. What was the original price?

A. 52 &nbsp; B. 57.60 &nbsp; C. 58 &nbsp; D. 60 &nbsp; E. 68

**Item 2.** Ana can paint a fence in 6 hours. Ben can paint the same fence in 3 hours. Working together at those rates, how long will it take them?

A. 1.5 hours &nbsp; B. 2 hours &nbsp; C. 2.5 hours &nbsp; D. 3 hours &nbsp; E. 4.5 hours

**Item 3.** In a class of 40 students, the ratio of boys to girls is 3 to 5. How many girls are in the class?

A. 15 &nbsp; B. 16 &nbsp; C. 24 &nbsp; D. 25 &nbsp; E. 30

**Item 4.** A product's price goes up 10%. A month later the new price goes down 10%. Compared with the starting price, the final price is:

A. the same &nbsp; B. 1% lower &nbsp; C. 1% higher &nbsp; D. 2% lower &nbsp; E. 10% lower

**Item 5.** A worker earns 15 an hour for the first 40 hours in a week and one and a half times that rate for every hour after 40. How much does the worker earn in a 46-hour week?

A. 690 &nbsp; B. 735 &nbsp; C. 750 &nbsp; D. 900 &nbsp; E. 1,035

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 5</summary>

**Item 1: D (60).** After a 20% cut you pay 80% of the original. So 0.8 × original = 48, and the original is 48 ÷ 0.8 = 60. Quick check: 20% of 60 is 12, and 60 minus 12 is 48. *Why not B:* 57.60 is 48 plus 20% of 48, which takes the percentage of the wrong number.

**Item 2: B (2 hours).** Ana does 1/6 of the fence an hour, Ben does 1/3, which is 2/6. Together they do 3/6, or half the fence, each hour. The whole fence takes 2 hours. *Why not E:* 4.5 hours is the average of their two times, but working together must be faster than Ben alone, which rules out anything of 3 hours or more.

**Item 3: D (25).** The ratio 3 to 5 splits the class into 3 + 5 = 8 equal parts. 40 ÷ 8 = 5 students per part. Girls are 5 parts: 25. *Why not C:* 24 is three fifths of 40, which uses the ratio as if it were a fraction of the whole.

**Item 4: B (1% lower).** Start with 100. Up 10% gives 110. Down 10% of 110 is 11, which gives 99. That is 1% below the start. *Why not A:* the two 10% changes are taken from different amounts, so they do not cancel.

**Item 5: B (735).** The first 40 hours pay 40 × 15 = 600. The overtime rate is 15 × 1.5 = 22.50, and 6 overtime hours pay 6 × 22.50 = 135. Total: 735. *Why not A:* 690 is 46 hours at the normal rate. *Why not E:* 1,035 is all 46 hours at the overtime rate.

</details>
