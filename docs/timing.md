# Timing and pacing

The cognitive section gives you 12 minutes for 50 questions. That is 720 seconds, or 14.4 seconds a question. Wonderlic itself describes its practice pace as "about 14 seconds each", and it says test takers are not expected to finish all 50. So pacing is not about reaching the end. It is about collecting as many right answers as you can before the clock stops.

## The budget

| | |
|---|---|
| Total time | 12 minutes |
| Questions | 50 |
| Average per question | About 14 seconds |
| Difficulty | Rises as the test goes on, according to Wonderlic |
| Order | Topics are mixed, not grouped |
| Skipping | Wonderlic advises against skipping around to come back later |

Two facts shape the plan. The early questions are the easier ones, so they should go faster than average. And coming back is not a safe strategy, so every question gets one visit: answer it or make your best guess, then move on.

## Time per question type

These are our study targets, not published figures.

| Question type | Target | Give up and guess after |
|---|---|---|
| [Vocabulary](sections/vocabulary.md) | 5 to 10 seconds | 15 seconds |
| [Analogies](sections/analogies.md) | 8 to 12 seconds | 15 seconds |
| [Number series](sections/number-series.md) | 10 to 15 seconds | 20 seconds |
| [Logic, two statements](sections/logic.md) | 10 to 15 seconds | 20 seconds |
| [Spatial](sections/spatial-reasoning.md) | 10 to 15 seconds | 20 seconds |
| [Math, short](sections/math-word-problems.md) | 10 to 15 seconds | 20 seconds |
| Math or logic puzzle, long | 25 to 40 seconds | 40 seconds, or guess at once if the test is nearly over |

## A pacing plan for 12 minutes

Glance at the clock at three checkpoints. These are guidance for a solid score, not a requirement to finish.

| Time used | Where you want to be |
|---|---|
| 3 minutes | Past question 15. The early items are the easy ones, so you should be ahead of the average pace here. |
| 6 minutes | Around question 25 to 28 |
| 9 minutes | Around question 35 |
| Last 30 seconds | Answer what you can in a few seconds each. Do not leave the current question blank. |

If you are well behind at the 3-minute mark, you are probably solving easy items too carefully. Trust your first clean answer on vocabulary and analogies.

## Where time usually goes

- **One long word problem.** A two-step price or work problem can quietly eat 45 seconds, the cost of three vocabulary items.
- **Rereading.** Reading the question once, slowly, is faster than reading it twice quickly.
- **Checking easy answers.** If the answer to a series item is in the options and fits every term, click it.
- **Doing exact arithmetic when an estimate would do.** If only one option is in the right range, you are finished.
- **Panic after a hard item.** One miss does not matter. Reset on the next question.

## Practise against the clock

1. Do each section guide's 5 items against the "fair target" time given there.
2. Then do mixed timed runs of 50 questions in 12 minutes, such as the [free one on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/wonderlic?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=wonderlic), so you get used to switching between types.
3. After each run, note your checkpoint positions and the slowest three questions. Those three are your pacing lesson for the day.
