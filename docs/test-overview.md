# The Wonderlic test explained

Most people who are asked to "take the Wonderlic" today are sent **Wonderlic Select**, an online pre-employment assessment with three parts. Only one part is timed and has right and wrong answers: a 12-minute cognitive ability test of 50 questions. That timed part is what people usually mean by "the Wonderlic", and it is what most of these notes prepare you for.

## Quick facts

| | |
|---|---|
| Publisher | Wonderlic, Inc. |
| Current assessment | Wonderlic Select (formerly called Wonscore) |
| Parts | Cognitive ability (timed), motivation (untimed), personality (untimed) |
| Cognitive section | 50 questions in 12 minutes |
| Motivation section | 58 items, untimed |
| Personality section | 150 items, untimed |
| Total time | About 35 minutes on average |
| Calculator | Not allowed |
| Finishing | Test takers are not expected to answer all 50 cognitive questions in the time |
| Pass mark | None. Wonderlic says there is no passing or failing grade |
| Your results | Only a Candidate Feedback Report, if the employer provides one. Numerical cognitive scores are not given to candidates |
| Cost to you | Free. Wonderlic does not sell its tests to individuals |
| Test link | Generally active for a maximum of 14 days |

Sources: [Wonderlic Select candidate FAQ](https://wonderlic.com/blog/candidates/wonderlic-select-test-frequently-asked-questions-from-candidates/), [Wonderlic test-taker hub](https://wonderlic.com/test-takers/) and [Wonderlic cognitive ability page](https://wonderlic.com/science/cognitive-ability/), checked 24 November 2026.

## The three parts of Wonderlic Select

Wonderlic says the assessment measures three things:

| Part | What Wonderlic says it measures | Timed? | Right or wrong answers? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cognitive ability | "your ability to learn, adapt, and solve problems" | Yes, 12 minutes | Yes |
| Motivation (vocational interests) | the types of work and activities you find most interesting and engaging | No | No |
| Personality | how you react to people, tasks and situations at work | No | No |

The results of the three parts are combined and compared with what a specific job demands. Wonderlic weights the cognitive score differently for different jobs. The [untimed sections page](untimed-sections.md) covers the motivation and personality parts.

## The cognitive section

Fifty multiple-choice questions in 12 minutes, which is about 14 seconds a question. Wonderlic's pages list these topics:

- simple math and word problems
- number series and pattern identification
- vocabulary and language comprehension
- analogies
- basic logic and logic puzzles
- spatial reasoning

Wonderlic says no single topic dominates, and that the questions get harder as the test goes on. The topics are mixed together rather than grouped into sections, so you might see a vocabulary item, then a word problem, then a series.

## How you take it

- **Online, on your own device,** with on-screen instructions before you start.
- **Usually unproctored.** Wonderlic says companies typically do not proctor it, though some choose to.
- **By invitation only.** The employer sends the link. Wonderlic cannot resend an invitation, so contact the employer if your link has expired or shows "Assessment not Available".
- **Accommodations** come through the employer, under the Americans with Disabilities Act. Give the employer as much information as you can, and they consult Wonderlic.
- **Retakes** are set by the employer, not by Wonderlic.

## Names you may hear

| Name | What it refers to |
|---|---|
| Wonderlic Select | The current three-part assessment |
| Wonscore | The former name of Wonderlic Select |
| The classic 50-question cognitive test | Wonderlic's own phrase for the traditional timed test, on which it says the average score is around 20 |
| Wonderlic Personnel Test, WPT, WPT-R | Older names for the cognitive test, still used by many prep sites. Wonderlic's current candidate pages do not use them |

Prep sites also mention shorter versions with different lengths. Wonderlic's current candidate pages do not describe them, so we do not give figures for them here.

## The NFL connection

The Wonderlic became widely known through American football. NFL teams used it at the Scouting Combine to assess draft prospects, and the league treated the scores as confidential: a 2012 NFL.com report quotes a memo from Commissioner Roger Goodell saying Wonderlic scores are "strictly confidential for club use only" ([NFL.com](https://www.nfl.com/news/wonderlic-scores-other-draft-info-is-confidential-goodell-says-09000d5d8281ad7a)). The scores you see online for individual players are leaks or reports, not official figures. Several news outlets reported in 2022 that the league had stopped using the test at the Combine, but we could not find a statement on an NFL or Wonderlic page to confirm it.

For a job applicant this history matters only in one way: it is why so much of what you read online about "good scores" is really about football players.

## The one rule that runs through the whole test

**Keep moving.** With about 14 seconds per question and harder items toward the end, the score comes from banking the quick marks and not getting stuck. Wonderlic's own advice is to "make your best guess and move on" when a question takes too long, and not to "skip around" hoping to come back. Every section guide in these notes ends with timing advice built on that rule, and the [timing guide](timing.md) puts it together.

## Practise the real format

Wonderlic offers a free official practice test on its [test-taker hub](https://wonderlic.com/test-takers/): 5 questions in 72 seconds, drawn from a larger pool so they change each time, with no sign-up. It is a good feel for the pace but too short to show your strengths.
